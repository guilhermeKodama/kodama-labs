# Validation Golden Rules

Grounded in: Zero to Sold (Kahl), The Lean Startup (Ries), The Mom Test (Fitzpatrick), Sprint (Knapp/GV).

---

## Time-box rules

| Rule | Source | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Prototype: max 5 days** | Sprint (Knapp/GV) | "Compress months of debate into a single week" — anything longer = scope creep |
| **First customer feedback: within 7 days** | Lean Startup (Ries) | Build-Measure-Learn loop must spin fast |
| **Decision gate: max 4 weeks per cycle** | Zero to Sold (Kahl) | Preparation stage ends with a clear go/no-go |
| **Kill bad ad creative: 72h** | Industry standard | No emotional attachment to underperformers |

## Build rules

1. **One assumption per test.** If the test fails, you must know exactly which assumption broke. Multiple variables = no learning.
2. **Smallest possible scope.** If you can cut a feature, cut it. The prototype validates the riskiest assumption only.
3. **No code if no-code works.** Tally + Sheets + Telegram bot beats custom backend for validation.
4. **No login, no payment, no dashboard during validation.** Each adds friction without testing the core hypothesis.
5. **Manual delivery is acceptable** (concierge MVP) as long as it doesn't bottleneck you to 5 users.

## Interview rules (The Mom Test)

1. **Talk about their life, not your idea.** "Tell me about the last time you..." beats "Would you use a product that..."
2. **Ask about past behavior, not future intentions.** "I would definitely buy that" is the most dangerous lie in startups.
3. **Look for facts, not opinions.** Compliments, hypotheticals, and wishlists are all worthless data.
4. **If a question won't change your business decision, don't ask it.**
5. **Listen more than you talk.** If you're pitching, you're not learning.

## Validation rules

1. **Define kill criteria before starting.** Specific numbers. "If CAC > R$ 400 across both channels, kill" — written down before you spend R$ 1.
2. **One channel = no signal.** Always run two channels (paid + organic, or Meta + Google) to separate hypothesis-failure from channel-failure.
3. **Paid traffic validates 4 things, not 2.** Problem, solution, reachability, and CAC. All four must clear.
4. **Vanity metrics are forbidden.** Registrations without engagement, email opens, time-on-page, follower counts.
5. **The single most important metric is the one closest to revenue.** Trial → paid conversion > registration → trial > visitor → registration. Optimize the one furthest down the funnel that you have data on.

## Money rules

1. **Minimum budget for paid signal: R$ 1,500-2,500.** Below this = noise, not data.
2. **Max viable CAC = LTV ÷ 4.** Anything tighter and the business won't survive operational costs.
3. **Don't charge before product-market signal.** Free during beta is fine; charging without delivery destroys trust.
4. **First paid users = signal, not revenue.** 5 paying customers means the model works; R$ 500 MRR is irrelevant.

## Anti-patterns (the killers)

1. **Falling in love with the idea.** Symptom: arguing with negative data. Defense: written kill criteria.
2. **Building before validating.** Symptom: "let me just add one more feature first." Defense: 5-day prototype max.
3. **Scope creep.** Symptom: "but what about Rio?" Defense: explicit "does NOT" list in the one-pager.
4. **Validation theater.** Symptom: friends saying "great idea!" Defense: only count behavior (paid clicks, completed onboarding, issuance), never opinions.
5. **Talking to the wrong people.** Symptom: mixed signals. Defense: filter ICP ruthlessly; reject leads outside the niche.
6. **Solving the founder's problem, not the market's.** Symptom: you're the persona. Defense: minimum 5 interviews with people unlike you before any build.

## The one rule that wins

**Speed of learning > everything else.** Every decision should optimize for: how fast do I get real-world data on the riskiest assumption?
